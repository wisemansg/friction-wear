## 📖 Project Overview

This laboratory project investigated the tribological behavior of engineering materials, coatings, and lubricants under controlled laboratory conditions. The work focused on understanding friction mechanisms, wear evolution, lubrication performance, and contact mechanics through a series of experimental tribological tests.

Tribology plays a critical role in engineering because friction and wear influence the reliability, efficiency, energy consumption, maintenance requirements, and lifespan of mechanical systems. Through laboratory-scale testing, engineers can evaluate material performance before deployment in real industrial applications.

The project combined theoretical tribology principles with practical experimentation to evaluate surface engineering solutions capable of reducing wear, minimizing friction, and improving component durability.

# ❓ QUESTION 1 (Tribological Testing & Wear Mechanisms)

## 🔍 How Laboratory Tribology Supports Surface Design

Tribological testing at laboratory scale provides valuable insight into the development of wear-resistant materials and coatings. By reproducing real operating conditions under controlled environments, engineers can evaluate friction behavior, wear mechanisms, and durability before industrial implementation.

Benefits include:

- Characterization of Coefficient of Friction (CoF)
- Determination of wear rates
- Identification of dominant wear mechanisms
- Optimization of coating selection
- Validation of lubrication strategies
- Prediction of component service life

---

## 🟠 Pin-on-Disc Method

The Pin-on-Disc test is one of the most widely used tribological testing methods. A stationary pin is pressed against a rotating disc while friction and wear are continuously measured.

### Parameters Controlled

- Normal load
- Sliding velocity
- Surface roughness
- Material hardness
- Disc rotational speed
- Contact temperature

### Typical ASTM G99 Dimensions

| Parameter | Typical Range |
|------------|------------|
| Pin Diameter | 2–10 mm |
| Disc Diameter | 30–100 mm |
| Disc Thickness | 2–10 mm |

### Wear Measurement Methods

- Mass loss
- LVDT displacement measurement
- Surface profilometry
- Optical microscopy
- Surface scanning

---

## 🔵 Disc-on-Disc Method

The Disc-on-Disc configuration evaluates wear and friction between two rotating discs under controlled loads.

Applications include:

- Gear contacts
- Bearings
- Clutches
- Rolling-sliding contacts
- Lubricated machine elements

Advantages:

- Simulates industrial contact geometries
- Evaluates lubrication regimes
- Studies oxidation effects
- Investigates conformal contacts

# 🧪 QUESTION 2 (Case Study 1: PVD Coating Comparison)

## 📊 Coatings Investigated

| Coating | Characteristics |
|----------|----------|
| AlTiCrN | Excellent oxidation resistance and low friction |
| AlTiN | Balanced wear and friction performance |
| TiN | High hardness but higher friction coefficient |

---

## 📈 Coefficient of Friction Analysis

Experimental observations revealed three distinct stages:

### Running-In Phase (0–10 m)

- Initial asperity interaction
- Surface adaptation
- Rapid CoF fluctuations

### Stabilization Phase (10–100 m)

- Formation of stable contact conditions
- Reduced CoF variation

### Steady-State Sliding

- Long-term friction behavior observed

### Results

- AlTiCrN exhibited the lowest friction coefficient
- AlTiCrN showed the most stable friction behavior
- TiN exhibited the highest CoF and largest fluctuations
- AlTiN demonstrated intermediate performance

---

## 📌 Engineering Interpretation

Lower CoF generally leads to:

- Reduced wear
- Lower heat generation
- Improved energy efficiency
- Longer tool life

Higher CoF results in:

- Increased surface damage
- Greater thermal stresses
- Accelerated wear progression

### Best Performing Coating

🏆 **AlTiCrN demonstrated the best tribological performance under dry sliding conditions.**

## 🎯 Objectives

The experiments aimed to:

- Compare lubricant performance
- Measure friction reduction
- Evaluate wear behavior
- Study lubricant chemistry
- Analyze wear debris formation

---

## 🔬 FTIR Oil Analysis

FTIR spectroscopy was used to characterize lubricant composition.

### Mineral Oil

Characteristics:

- Simpler hydrocarbon structure
- Fewer absorption peaks
- Limited additive package

### Synthetic Oil

Characteristics:

- More complex molecular structure
- Additional functional groups
- Presence of anti-wear additives
- Enhanced film strength

---

## 📉 Friction Performance Results

Results showed:

- Synthetic oil consistently produced lower friction coefficients
- Friction reduction occurred at both load levels
- Lubricating film remained more stable under increasing load

### Engineering Explanation

Synthetic oils provide:

- Better thermal stability
- Stronger lubricant films
- Improved additive chemistry
- Enhanced anti-wear performance

---

## 🧮 Holm–Archard Wear Model

Wear volume was estimated using:

V = K × (Fₙ / H) × d

Where:

- V = Wear volume
- K = Wear coefficient
- Fₙ = Normal load
- H = Material hardness
- d = Sliding distance

### Results

- Synthetic oil generated lower wear volumes
- Wear increased with increasing load
- Lubricant chemistry significantly influenced wear resistance

---

## 🧱 Hertzian Contact Analysis

The Hertzian theory was applied to evaluate stresses generated at contacting surfaces.

### Parameters Investigated

- Contact pressure
- von Mises stress
- Subsurface shear stress
- Stress distribution

### Software Used

**HertzWin**

The simulations enabled visualization of:

- Contact stress concentration
- Elastic deformation
- Stress propagation below the surface

---

## 🔬 Surface & Wear Debris Analysis

Microscopy and EDS analysis were used to examine wear tracks and debris particles.

### Observations

- Evidence of adhesive wear
- Oxidative wear products identified
- Material transfer between contacting surfaces
- Debris morphology correlated with wear severity

EDS analysis provided elemental information that helped identify oxidation products and transferred material.
